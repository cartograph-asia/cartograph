Cartograph is a photo survey of South, Southeast and East Asia, made by the people who live and travel there.

The land is divided into plots of about 0.74 km² each. A Surveyor takes a photo inside a plot and uploads it. The first photo claims the plot, and its Surveyor stays its first surveyor for good. Everyone who comes later adds to the plot's story: another season, another street, the bridge after it was rebuilt.

Coverage is the share of a region's plots that have at least one photo. We start with Thailand.

How to survey

  1. Open the map and find a blank plot near you.
  2. Take a photo there. The plot is read from the photo's location.
  3. Sign in with your e-mail address (there is no password) and upload it.

That is all. There are no streaks to keep and nothing to buy. Every photo you upload is published under CC BY-SA 4.0, so the survey stays open for everyone.

Where the idea comes from

Geograph Britain and Ireland has collected photos of every corner of those islands since 2005. Cartograph tries the same for Asia, on a map built from OpenStreetMap.

Who runs it

Cartograph is a small, independent project. It follows a short list of principles: people first, newest first, no tracking, no growth pressure. The code is on GitHub. Write to hello@cartograph.asia.
