# Principles

Cartograph is a community that happens to have a very good map – not a map app with users. These principles decide feature questions. When a proposal conflicts with one of them, the principle wins unless Adrian says otherwise.

1. **People first, map second.** The home page shows what Surveyors did, not a map. The map is one view among several, reached from the content.
2. **Chronological, not algorithmic.** Streams show the newest things first. No engagement ranking, no "for you", no hidden sorting. Visitors always know where they are and why they see something.
3. **Named people with pages.** Every Surveyor has a profile page: their plots, their map, their photos, their story. A profile is a place, not a counter.
4. **Conversation at the object.** Comments live under the photo and the plot. Local knowledge ("this bridge was rebuilt in 2024") is a contribution, not noise.
5. **Open by default.** Nothing requires an account to view. Photos carry a Creative Commons licence. Every region has an RSS feed. Data can be exported. No tracking, so no cookie banner.
6. **No growth pressure.** No push notifications designed to pull people back, no streaks that punish absence, no dark patterns. A weekly digest e-mail is the loudest Cartograph gets.
7. **Fast and quiet.** Pages render on the server and are usable before any script runs. Big photos, generous whitespace, good type, dense with content and empty of decoration. If a page needs a spinner, it is doing too much.
8. **Earned visibility.** Ranks and standings reflect work done in the field. Nothing can be bought, boosted or gamed by volume alone.

Early-web spirit, current-day craft: the community feeling of 2005 without its tables, frames, counters and forced logins.
